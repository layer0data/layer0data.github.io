What changes when data is active, not passive

One root cause behind fifty years of spending: only the places around data get protected — never the data itself. Change that, and everything below follows.

  • Data that expires on the day you decided — at its birth — no matter where the copy lives.
  • Not everything deserves to be stored. Duplicates and junk are refused at the door, and every AI answer gets a choice at birth — keep it governed, keep it briefly, or leave no trace. 68% of the data available to businesses goes unleveraged (Seagate/IDC) — kept, paid for, never used.
  • Your most confidential data finally used with AI — because the AI needs permission, the answer follows the source's rules, and nothing escapes ungoverned.
  • A stolen laptop, a copied disk, a leaked archive — worth nothing to the thief. The leak-extortion economy simply has nothing to sell.
  • Every file carries its own papers — who created it, who changed it, who opened it, who was refused — provable, travelling with the object. In a world of fakes, the thing with papers wins.
  • Delete actually means delete — everywhere a governed copy went, including what AI made from it.
  • Less spent defending, more spent building. Security stops being a product you keep buying out of fear and becomes a property your data has.
